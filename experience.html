The thread

Different industries, the same work. Dental devices, horticulture technology, marketing software, bridal aftercare. Each time the lever that moved the most was one nobody outside could see: a price, a product line that had to go, a process that had never been measured.

Finding that lever, then pulling it properly, is what I have always been paid for.

Scale-up

Triodent

A New Zealand dental company that went from ten staff to more than a hundred while I ran marketing. That meant building a sales and marketing team of over twenty and a brand to match where the business was heading. The biggest lever was not either of those. A pricing overhaul protected the business from a strong US dollar and contributed to 300% revenue growth in twelve months.

And the harder call: cutting around 60% of the range so the flagship could have the focus. Good products, decent margins, and still the wrong ones to keep. The flagship doubled to around $40m in under two years.

evoSuite

Took a booking tool built for tourism operators and repositioned it as a marketing automation platform for a much bigger market. Worked with MBIE to bring UCLA in on the groundwork for a US launch.

Before that

I started on the technical side, running a service department, and found that the problems worth fixing were in how it quoted and sold rather than how it repaired. Three months on sales and quoting lifted revenue and output with no extra payroll.

In 2001 I started my first company, an IT firm that grew from one customer to more than 120, then moved across into sales and marketing for good. In 2010 the New Zealand Government sent me to MIT Sloan's Entrepreneurship Development Program, and I have used what I learnt there in every role since.
