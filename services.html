Most growth problems are not specialist problems. They are decisions nobody has made yet. So the useful question is not which service you are buying, it is how far in I need to be.

Where I do the work

Commercial, systems, and how the business runs

  • Pricing
  • Offer and packaging
  • Productising a service
  • Margin
  • Operating rhythm
  • Reporting and decision-making
  • Process and systems

This is the part I am actually expert in, and the part most businesses skip because it is invisible from the outside. It is also where the biggest numbers usually are. Changing what you sell and what you keep moves more than changing a headline.

Where I get you started

Marketing, website, SEO, email, ads

  • Channel strategy
  • Website and landing pages
  • SEO foundations
  • Email and lifecycle
  • Paid search and social
  • Conversion

Plenty of businesses have none of this, or have it badly. Going from nothing to working is not specialist work, and I will do it with you: set the strategy, build the foundations, get something live and measured.

I am not going to tell you I am an SEO expert. I run SEO for my own business and I can take yours from nothing to noticeably better. Getting from good to best in any one channel is somebody else's job, and knowing where that line sits is the point of this section.

Where I bring in a specialist

Choosing them, briefing them, holding them to it

When a channel genuinely needs an expert, the valuable thing is not pretending to be one. It is knowing that the moment has arrived, picking someone good, writing a brief worth answering, and checking the work against numbers that mean something.

I keep a short list of people I would use on my own business, because I do. Email past the basics goes to an agency I have worked with for years. Paid search goes to someone who does nothing else. You get the specialist and someone in your corner who can tell whether they are earning the fee.

That is usually the cheapest version of this whole conversation. Most businesses are not underserved by specialists. They are over-sold by them, in the wrong order, before anyone worked out what the actual problem was.
