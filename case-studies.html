Selling a quote as a product

MyDressboxProduct strategyCR

Problem

Getting a wedding dress cleaned meant carrying it round town. Three dry cleaners, three counters, three people looking at it and naming a different price. Nobody could buy without a conversation, and nobody could buy at all unless they lived near a cleaner who would touch bridal.

Built

Five fixed prices, $299 to $599, bought online like anything else. The box arrives at her door, the gown goes back in it, it comes home cleaned and boxed.

The point

The tier keys off the gown, not the dirt. Style, length, circumference, volume, detailing. That is what makes a price quotable from a photo instead of a counter, and it is what turned a local service into one that sells to the whole country.

The hard part was never the website. It was deciding what varies and what does not, then being willing to fix a price on a gown nobody has seen yet.

Dress|OS

MyDressboxSystemsTL

Problem

Gowns moved through the laundry on a paper log. Nobody could say where a dress was without walking the floor. How long each stage took was written down nowhere. Several subscriptions were each doing part of the job and none of them talked to each other.

Method

Scoped the process that already existed, on the floor, with the people running it. Documented it as eleven stages. Built it. Connected the systems already being paid for, Shopify, AusPost, Supabase, Google. Cancelled what the build replaced.

Result

The floor runs off phones. Queue counts are live across all eleven stages. Every stage with real work in it is timed, so there is finally an answer to what a gown costs in minutes.

The software is not the output. The measurements are. You cannot fix a process nobody has ever timed.

Cognito Crayons

In progressCosting, brand, storefrontTL

Problem

A crayon manufacturer with 64 recipes and no reliable idea what any of them cost to make. Pricing came off a spreadsheet several people had edited over several years. Ingredients, labour and overhead all sat apart, so nobody could answer the only question that matters. What does one crayon cost.

Built

Cray|OS. Ingredients at their real prices, labour rates by task, overhead, and a recipe builder that puts the three together. Change an ingredient price and every recipe using it moves.

Also

Brand and a Shopify storefront alongside it, built for two buyers with nothing in common. A parent arrives from social with ninety seconds and needs to believe the colour is strong and the stick survives a fist. A teacher needs something requisitionable: a price break, a printable programme, a GST invoice.

The sharpest call in the brand was a list of banned words. Natural, plant based, food grade, edible. The whole vocabulary the category reaches for by reflex, and every one of them invites a parent to think of a crayon as food. It is certified non toxic and boring enough that it will not stay in a mouth. A drawing tool, not a snack.

No percentages here. Some of this moved numbers I am happy to take you through with the working. I am not going to print a figure on a web page you have no way of checking. If a consultancy's case studies are all round numbers pointing up and to the right, ask them the same question.

Start with the diagnosis.

If the useful part above was the reasoning rather than the result, that is the part worth buying.
